Textual Parallel & Philosophical Inheritance

👴🏼 John Locke, Second Treatise (1689)
"Men being, as has been said, by nature all free, equal, and independent..."

Section 95: Discussing the natural state before civil society.

🦅 Thomas Jefferson, Declaration of Independence (1776)
"We hold these truths to be self-evident, that all men are created equal..."

Preamble: Universal justification for dissolving political bonds.

🔍 Historical & Conceptual Analysis

Jefferson did not write in a vacuum; his declaration was an intentional distillation of the "common sense of the subject." He directly transcribed Locke's core Enlightenment axiom that all individuals enter the world with natural parity.

DBQ Essay Prompts & Teacher Discussion Guide

Use these questions for class discussion or student essay scaffolding:

Primary Prompt

To what extent was the Declaration of Independence a direct application of Lockean social contract theory?

Look closely at Locke's definition of "consent" and the "Right of Revolution" when legislators destroy property or exercise arbitrary power.

Textual Shift Analysis

Why did Jefferson substitute "the pursuit of Happiness" for Locke's original triad of "Life, Liberty, and Estate (Property)"?

Consider the broader moral connotations of Greek/Scottish eudaimonia (virtue & public flourishing) vs. narrow landed wealth.

Hobbesian Counter-Argument

How would Thomas Hobbes criticize the American colonists' decision to declare independence?

Hobbes argued that fracturing the "common power" dissolves civil society back into the brutal "war of every man against every man."
